Conventions

Channels and matrix layout

Each orbital $a$ carries four bilinears, one per entry of directions: N (0), X (1), Y (2), Z (3), from the spin matrices $\sigma^0, \sigma^x, \sigma^y, \sigma^z$. Every matrix RPAToolkit works with, $\chi(q)$ and $V(q)$ alike, is d * length(directions) square, with the channel index innermost: orbital $a$, channel $\mu$ sits at row length(directions) * (a - 1) + μ. Without spin-orbit coupling $\chi$ is block diagonal in the channel.

The bare susceptibility

combine_chis assembles the stored bubbles into $\chi_{ij}(q) = \langle O_i(-q) O_j(q) \rangle$ (a plain transpose of what TRIQS's contraction gives), normalized as the per-spin bubble $\chi_0$ of Graser et al., NJP 11, 025016 (2009) (twice what is stored). CHI_ORIENTATION names this convention, and run_rpa records it in its output. The static bubble is positive semidefinite.

When the model is spin-independent the four channels of $\chi$ are identical. The channel slots then only acquire a meaning through the interaction matrix.

RPA and the instability

\[\chi_\mathrm{RPA}(q) = \left[1 - \chi(q)\, s V(q)\right]^{-1} \chi(q)\]

for an interaction $V$ at strength $s$. Because $\chi \ge 0$, a positive eigen-direction of $\chi V$ is enhanced and a negative one is screened. The critical strength is the smallest $s$ at which $\chi_\mathrm{RPA}$ acquires a negative eigenvalue at some $q$, i.e. $s \lambda_\mathrm{max} = 1$ for the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_\mathrm{max}$ of $\chi(q) V(q)$ over all $q$. find_instability brackets it by bisection on instability.lower/upper (resolution $(\text{upper} - \text{lower}) / 2^{32}$). It reports $\chi_\mathrm{RPA}$ just below it, whose largest eigenvalue sits at the ordering wavevector ("maximum momentum"). A case that never goes unstable reports upper.

The screened interaction is $V + V \chi_\mathrm{RPA} V$ (effective_interaction).

Interaction slots

With that sign convention:

  • slot 1 (N) holds the spin vertex $U^{(s)}$. A positive entry is Stoner-enhanced.
  • slot 4 (Z) holds minus the charge vertex, $-U^{(c)}$. A repulsive charge interaction is negative there and screened.
  • X and Y stay empty without spin-orbit coupling.

The two building blocks:

termchannelsconstant
on-site Hubbard $U n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$$\mathrm{diag}(+1, 0, 0, -1)$: $U^{(s)} = U^{(c)} = U$HUBBARD_CHANNELS
density-density $V n_a n_b$$\mathrm{diag}(0, 0, 0, -1)$: charge onlyDENSITY_CHANNELS

With DENSITY_CHANNELS, an on-site $U$ plus a tail $V(q) = \sum_\delta e^{-iq\cdot\delta} V_\delta$ gives $U^{(c)}(q) = U + V(q)$. Graser's density-density vertex is $2V$; the multi-orbital ZrNCl builder in the TMD project registers diag(0, 0, 0, -2) for that reason. The two differ only in what is called $V$.

Hermitian conjugates

interaction adds every registered bond and its Hermitian conjugate. Two consequences:

  • Register one direction per inter-site bond. TightBindingToolkit's Lookup merges $(i, j, R)$ with $(j, i, -R)$, so registering both counts the bond twice.
  • An on-site bond lands in its own block twice. Register U/2 .* HUBBARD_CHANNELS to get $U$. hubbard_coulomb_interactions does this.

The interaction strength $s$ multiplies the whole interaction file. A case's critical $U$ is $s_c$ times the $U$ the file was built with.

The bilinear basis

By default (bilinear_basis: per_orbital) the operators are the per-orbital densities and spins, one per orbital. That set is closed under the point group only when the symmetry operations permute orbitals. That holds for one orbital per site (square, triangular, honeycomb), but not for an E doublet, where $\chi_{ij}$ cannot be covariant.

bilinear_basis: closed replaces it with the span of the densities closed under the measured orbital representation (bilinear_closure). That span is larger only where symmetry forces it. Both stages must use it:

  1. The interaction builder projects its vertex onto the same span: kanamori_vertex → project_vertex → channel_blocks. It saves "bilinear_basis" and one constant matrix per case in "closed_matrices".
  2. run_bare contracts the bubble onto it and stores the basis in each .npz.
  3. run_rpa measures the residual rotation between the two frames (basis_rotation) and applies it. It refuses mixed per-orbital/closed inputs, and skips cases with no closed-basis matrix (a $q$-dependent tail has none).
