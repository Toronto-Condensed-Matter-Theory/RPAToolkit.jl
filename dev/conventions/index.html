Conventions

Channels and matrix layout

Each orbital $a$ carries four bilinears, one per entry of directions: N (0), X (1), Y (2), Z (3), from the spin matrices $\sigma^0, \sigma^x, \sigma^y, \sigma^z$. Every matrix RPAToolkit works with, $\chi(q)$ and $V(q)$ alike, is d * length(directions) square, with the channel index innermost: orbital $a$, channel $\mu$ sits at row length(directions) * (a - 1) + μ. Without spin-orbit coupling $\chi$ is block diagonal in the channel; with it, the channels mix (see Spin-orbit coupling and spin sectors).

The bare susceptibility

combine_chis assembles the stored bubbles into $\chi_{ij}(q)$, the response of the $e^{iq\cdot R}$ component of $O_i$ to a field $e^{iq\cdot R}$ on $O_j$ ($R$ the lattice vectors, in TightBindingToolkit's $H(k) = \sum_R e^{ik\cdot R} t(R)$). The interaction is built in the same Bloch convention, $V_{ij}(q) = \sum M e^{+iq\cdot\delta}$ per bond (INTERACTION_CONVENTION), which is what makes $(1 - \chi V)^{-1}\chi$ the RPA. Both are checked against an exact real-space Hartree RPA on a periodic cluster. That is a plain transpose, of the whole matrix, of what the bare stage stores (stored_bubble is the transpose of the Lindhard sum with the operators transposed), normalized as the per-spin bubble $\chi_0$ of Graser et al., NJP 11, 025016 (2009) (twice what is stored). CHI_ORIENTATION names this convention, and run_rpa records it in its output. The static bubble is positive semidefinite.

When the model is spin-independent the four channels of $\chi$ are identical. The channel slots then only acquire a meaning through the interaction matrix.

RPA and the instability

\[\chi_\mathrm{RPA}(q) = \left[1 - \chi(q)\, s V(q)\right]^{-1} \chi(q)\]

for an interaction $V$ at strength $s$. Because $\chi \ge 0$, a positive eigen-direction of $\chi V$ is enhanced and a negative one is screened. The critical strength is the smallest $s$ at which $\chi_\mathrm{RPA}$ acquires a negative eigenvalue at some $q$, i.e. $s \lambda_\mathrm{max} = 1$ for the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_\mathrm{max}$ of $\chi(q) V(q)$ over all $q$. find_instability brackets it by bisection on instability.lower/upper (resolution $(\text{upper} - \text{lower}) / 2^{32}$). It reports $\chi_\mathrm{RPA}$ just below it, whose largest eigenvalue sits at the ordering wavevector ("maximum momentum"). A case that never goes unstable reports upper.

The screened interaction is $V + V \chi_\mathrm{RPA} V$ (effective_interaction).

Interaction slots

With that sign convention, the slots are the physical channels (VERTEX_SLOTS):

  • slot N holds minus the charge vertex, $-U^{(c)}$. A repulsive charge interaction is negative there and screened.
  • slots X, Y and Z each hold the spin vertex $U^{(s)}$. A positive entry is Stoner-enhanced.

Written in the Pauli channels, the antisymmetrized Hubbard vertex is exactly $U\,\mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$, so this layout is the full spinful RPA, and stays right when spin-orbit coupling makes $\chi$ mix the channels.

The two building blocks:

termchannelsconstant
on-site Hubbard $U n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$$\mathrm{diag}(-1, +1, +1, +1)$: $U^{(s)} = U^{(c)} = U$HUBBARD_CHANNELS
density-density $V n_a n_b$$\mathrm{diag}(-1, 0, 0, 0)$: charge onlyDENSITY_CHANNELS

With DENSITY_CHANNELS, an on-site $U$ plus a tail $V(q) = \sum_\delta e^{+iq\cdot\delta} V_\delta$ gives $U^{(c)}(q) = U + V(q)$. Graser's density-density vertex is $2V$; the multi-orbital ZrNCl builder in the TMD project registers diag(-2, 0, 0, 0) for that reason. The two differ only in what is called $V$.

Interaction files record the layout as "vertex_slots" => VERTEX_SLOTS, and run_rpa refuses a file without it.

Until 2026-11-12 interaction built $V(q)$ with $e^{-iq\cdot\delta}$, which is $V(-q) = V(q)^T$. That is the same thing for on-site interactions and on lattices with one site per cell, but on a multi-site cell a non-local interaction entered the RPA transposed; the honeycomb example's $1/r$ charge response was off by 30-40% (its critical strengths, set by the local spin vertex, were not). run_rpa now records "interaction_convention" => INTERACTION_CONVENTION in its output. Until 2026-11-01 slot N held $U^{(s)}$ and slot Z held $-U^{(c)}$, with X and Y empty: the same physics without spin-orbit coupling (critical strengths are identical, and the screened interaction's N and Z blocks swap), but not with it.

Spin-orbit coupling and spin sectors

How many entries of the $4 \times 4$ channel matrix $\chi^{\mu\nu}$ can be nonzero is set by the spin rotations the hopping model commutes with. spin_symmetry and the bare stage (band_spin_symmetry) apply the same exact tests to the serialized hoppings:

symmetrythe modelbubblechannel pairssectors
SU2$H = h \otimes 1$, no spin-orbit couplingspinless $\times \tfrac12$NN = XX = YY = ZZcharge (N); spin (Z, with X and Y identical copies)
U1zno spin-flip hopping, up $\ne$ down (Ising)spinfulNN, NZ, ZN, ZZ; XX, XY, YX, YYlongitudinal (N, Z); transverse (X, Y)
noneanything elsespinfulall sixteenone

$S_z$ conservation only connects operators of equal $S_z$ charge, which is why N and Z (charge 0) and X and Y (charges $\pm 1$) form separate sectors. The cross terms are real physics: a Zeeman field gives $\chi^{NZ}$, and Ising spin-orbit coupling separates $\chi^{+-}$ from $\chi^{-+}$, i.e. gives $\chi^{XY}$. With time reversal and one band, $\chi^{NZ}$ vanishes.

The bare stage contracts only the pairs the symmetry allows, and stores which one it used as spin_symmetry in each .npz. A U1z run also contracts the pairs it skips once, at the first chemical potential, and stops if they are not zero. bubble_spin: full forces the spinful bubble and all sixteen pairs, which checks the reduced routes. combine_chis places every stored pair, and refuses a file missing one its symmetry needs. The RPA is then solved one sector at a time (spin_sectors, sector_plan). An interaction that is not block diagonal in the model's sectors falls back to the whole matrix. find_instability reports which sector goes unstable.

bilinear_basis: closed needs an SU2 model: the closure measures the orbital representation from one spin block, which is not the symmetry of a spin-orbit-coupled model.

Hermitian conjugates

interaction adds every registered bond and its Hermitian conjugate. Two consequences:

  • Register one direction per inter-site bond. TightBindingToolkit's Lookup merges $(i, j, R)$ with $(j, i, -R)$, so registering both counts the bond twice.
  • An on-site bond lands in its own block twice. Register U/2 .* HUBBARD_CHANNELS to get $U$. hubbard_coulomb_interactions does this.

The interaction strength $s$ multiplies the whole interaction file. A case's critical $U$ is $s_c$ times the $U$ the file was built with.

The bilinear basis

By default (bilinear_basis: per_orbital) the operators are the per-orbital densities and spins, one per orbital. That set is closed under the point group only when the symmetry operations permute orbitals. That holds for one orbital per site (square, triangular, honeycomb), but not for an E doublet, where $\chi_{ij}$ cannot be covariant.

bilinear_basis: closed replaces it with the span of the densities closed under the measured orbital representation (bilinear_closure). That span is larger only where symmetry forces it. Both stages must use it:

  1. The interaction builder projects its vertex onto the same span: kanamori_vertex → project_vertex → channel_blocks. It saves "bilinear_basis" and one constant matrix per case in "closed_matrices".
  2. run_bare contracts the bubble onto it and stores the basis in each .npz.
  3. run_rpa measures the residual rotation between the two frames (basis_rotation) and applies it. It refuses mixed per-orbital/closed inputs, and skips cases with no closed-basis matrix (a $q$-dependent tail has none).
