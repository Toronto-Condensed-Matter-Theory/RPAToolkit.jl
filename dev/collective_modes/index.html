Collective modes and mean-field theory

The static RPA finds where an instability sets in. At real frequency the same RPA describes the collective modes of the state it is built on: magnons of a magnet, plasmons of a metal. Built on a Hartree-Fock state with the vertex of the same interaction, it is the linear response of that state, time-dependent Hartree-Fock (TDHF).

The real-frequency bubble

dynamic_bubble returns the retarded bubble $\chi_0(q, \omega + i\eta)$, one 4d × 4d matrix per momentum and frequency, in the static pipeline's layout (channel innermost, per-spin normalization, Conventions). At $\omega = 0$ it is exactly what run_rpa assembles from the stored static bubble, the same discrete sum even for momenta off the k grid, so every interaction matrix built for the static RPA applies unchanged. It is a proper response matrix: the absorptive part $(\chi - \chi^\dagger)/2i$ is $\mathrm{sign}(\omega)$ times a positive semidefinite matrix.

Two ways of doing the frequency sum:

  • nbins = nothing: every pole exactly. Costs one term per band pair and frequency.
  • nbins = N (default 4000): the pole weights are spread linearly onto $N$ energies spanning the particle-hole range, then one matrix product gives all frequencies. The error is O(bin width / $\eta$).

The momenta are arbitrary (a path), the k sum runs over a k_size × k_size grid, and the work is threaded over momenta. The broadening $\eta$ has to exceed the level spacing the grid resolves near the momenta of interest, roughly $v_F \cdot 2\pi/(k_\mathrm{size}\, a)$.

dynamic_rpa solves $\chi_\mathrm{RPA} = (1 - \chi_0 V)^{-1} \chi_0$ at every point; particle_hole_edges gives the upper edge of the particle-hole continuum (the support of $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_0$ before broadening), above which a collective mode is undamped.

Spectral functions and supercells

A magnetically ordered state lives on a supercell, whose Brillouin zone folds the original one. unfold_vector writes the original lattice's operator at momentum $q$, $\sum_r e^{-iq\cdot r} c^\dagger_r \sigma^a c_r/2$, in the supercell's per-site channels, $u_{(i,a)} = e^{+iq\cdot b_i}/\sqrt{n}$, so that $u^\dagger \chi u$ is the original lattice's susceptibility at $q$ (pinned numerically: a paramagnetic two-site cell unfolds onto the one-site bubble). spectral_map does this along a path, and rpa_poles reads off the peak at each momentum.

Long-range Coulomb

A two-dimensional plasmon needs $V(q) \propto 1/|q|$ as $q \to 0$, which no real-space tail truncated at a finite range has. coulomb_2d Ewald-sums $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r \ne r'} e^2/(\varepsilon|r - r'|)\, n_r n_{r'}$ exactly (its value does not depend on the Ewald split, to $10^{-13}$). It returns $-2 v(q)$ in the charge slot: the factor 2 because $\chi$ is the per-spin bubble, so that the RPA reads $1 + 2 v \chi_0 = 1 + v \Pi$ with $\Pi$ the full density bubble, and the 2D electron gas gives $\omega_p^2 = 2\pi n e^2 q/(\varepsilon m)$.

Hartree-Fock bands: the MeanFieldToolkit interface

A converged MeanFieldToolkit TBMFTModel hands its state to the RPA:

using RPAToolkit, MeanFieldToolkit
# ... build `mft`, then sc = SolveMFT!(mft, initial; Update = AndersonMixing, ...) ...
r = rpa_model(mft; residual = maximum(abs, sc.VOuts[end] - sc.VIns[end]))
V = rpa_vertex(mft)                         # q -> V(q)
chi0 = dynamic_bubble(r.bands, qs, omegas, k_size; beta = r.beta, mu = r.mu, eta = 0.02)
chi = dynamic_rpa(chi0, V.(qs))

rpa_model returns the BandModel of the Hartree-Fock unit cell (the free hoppings plus the decoupled interaction), with its chemical potential and temperature. rpa_vertex builds the vertex from the same interaction tensor MeanFieldToolkit decoupled. For an on-site term $\sum U_{abcd}\, c^\dagger_a c_b c^\dagger_c c_d$, that is the derivative of its Hartree-Fock self-energy, $K_{abcd} = U_{abcd} + U_{cdab} - U_{cbad} - U_{adcb}$ (MeanFieldToolkit's IntraQuarticToHopping kernel), projected on the channels (quartic_onsite_vertex, tdhf_channel_vertex). For $U n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$ it is exactly HUBBARD_CHANNELS.

Because the bands and the vertex come from one mean-field functional, a spontaneously broken continuous symmetry gives an exact Goldstone mode. For the Néel state of the Hubbard model, the largest transverse eigenvalue of $\chi_0 V$ at the ordering vector is 1 to all printed digits, at every $U$ (examples/MeanField/Magnons.jl, test/test_meanfield_ext.jl). The offset scales with the self-consistency residual, which is why rpa_model checks it: MeanFieldToolkit builds the final unit cell from the last input order parameters.

Inter-site interactions (InterQuarticToHopping) enter with their Hartree part (quartic_hartree_vertex). Their exchange part renormalizes the Hartree-Fock bands (a nearest-neighbour $V$ widens the band). That part is a bond term with no representation on per-site channels, so it is left out of the RPA, and that part of the RPA is not the linear response of the mean-field state; rpa_vertex warns. rpa_vertex(mft; interactions = [...]) keeps only some terms, e.g. to take the on-site vertex from the mean-field theory and the long-range tail from coulomb_2d.

Worked examples

  • examples/MeanField/Magnons.jl: square-lattice Hubbard, Néel order. The zone-boundary magnon is 0.71, 0.91 and 0.96 of linear spin-wave theory's $2J = 8t^2/U$ at $U = 4, 8, 12$, and the zone-boundary dispersion has the sign of the higher-order (ring-exchange) correction, $\omega(\pi/2, \pi/2) < \omega(\pi, 0)$.
  • examples/MeanField/Plasmons.jl: a dilute metal ($n = 0.1$) with $e^2/\varepsilon r$. The plasmon follows $\sqrt{2\pi n e^2 q/m^*}$ at small $q$ ($m^*$ the Hartree-Fock mass, 2% lighter than the bare one through the Fock term), disperses faster at larger $q$, and is Landau-damped once it meets the particle-hole continuum.
